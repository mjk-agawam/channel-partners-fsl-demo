## Service Lines

### 1. Break-Fix & Reactive Service

**Description:**
- Display repairs at retail locations
- Equipment troubleshooting
- Emergency response
- Parts ordering, approval, installation

**Workflow:**
1. Rep or call center identifies issue
2. Troubleshoot remotely if possible
3. Order part (approval required for high-value items)
4. Ship to store location OR rep's home
5. Schedule installation visit
6. Complete work, capture photos/signature
7. Job costing tracked by project

**Volume:**
- ~500 dedicated break-fix reps
- Unknown % of shared team work
- Reactive by nature (can't be pre-scheduled)

**Pain Points:**
- Manual parts ordering via call center
- Go-backs due to wrong parts or incomplete troubleshooting
- No automated escalation when merch rep spots an issue
- No preventive maintenance capability

### 2. Installation & Construction

**Description:**
- Multi-person teams (5-15 reps)
- Multi-day projects at single location
- Fixture installations, store remodels, heavy equipment setup
- Requires hard scheduling (manager assigns exact date/time)

**Workflow:**
1. Client service manager creates project
2. Configure requirements (crew size, skills, duration)
3. Check availability across team members
4. Assign reps and push schedule to their calendars
5. Coordinate travel (often overnight, cross-country)
6. Team arrives on-site, completes work over multiple days
7. Daily timekeeping and expense tracking
8. Job costing by project

**Volume:**
- ~300 construction/installation specialists
- Frequent travel required (teams fly in from multiple regions)

**Pain Points:**
- Team scheduling is manual and complex (availability, skills, geography)
- Travel spend is "insane" (opportunity to reduce with better local scheduling)
- Hard to reschedule when one team member calls in sick

### 3. Merchandising & Resets

**Description:**
- Shelf resets and planogram execution
- Product placement and cleaning
- Inventory movement (not restocking — store staff does that)
- Routine store visits (weekly, bi-weekly, monthly)

**Workflow:**
1. Client service manager creates wave (date range, store list)
2. Reps self-schedule within wave window
3. System forces consolidation (multiple visits at same store → same day)
4. Nightly route optimization (re-swizzle appointments for efficiency)
5. Rep checks in at store, completes survey (highly conditional)
6. Photos, product tracking, compliance verification
7. Check out, time entry, sync data

**Volume:**
- ~2,500 merchandising reps (largest LOB)
- 1,900 stores per week just for Target alone
- Mix of dedicated (single brand) and flex (multi-brand)

**Pain Points:**
- Self-scheduling is "people-friendly" but CEO challenges it due to cost
- No cross-LOB work (merch rep can't create break-fix ticket)
- Survey creation is complex (though very flexible)
- No historical context for reps (prior visits, defects, contacts)

### 4. Audits & Compliance

**Description:**
- Store compliance verification
- Competitive intelligence (check competitor displays)
- Mystery shopping-style assessments
- Product presence and placement tracking

**Workflow:**
- Similar to merchandising (survey-driven)
- Highly configurable question sets
- Photo capture required
- Data flows to client dashboards

**Volume:**
- Mix of dedicated teams and shared audit specialists
- Often bundled with merchandising visits

**Pain Points:**
- Survey complexity (though OpenSky handles this well)
- No image recognition / AI validation (manual photo review)

### 5. Assisted Sales & Training

**Description:**
- Product demonstrations in-store
- Training of retail store associates
- Brand advocacy and relationship building
- Sales presentations and materials distribution

**Workflow:**
1. Dedicated brand reps (LG, Samsung, etc.) visit assigned stores
2. Access training materials via mobile app (PDFs, videos)
3. Interact with store associates or customers
4. Capture training completion or sales activity
5. Relationship management (SRM module)

**Volume:**
- Mostly dedicated brand teams (~800 reps)
- High-touch, relationship-focused work

**Pain Points:**
- Training content is in Resources section (not contextual)
- No CRM integration (sales pipeline → field execution)
- No upsell/cross-sell workflow

### 6. Warehousing & Logistics

**Description:**
- 60K sq ft warehouse facility
- Parts fulfillment for break-fix work
- Kitting and shipping for installations
- Inventory management

**Workflow:**
1. Parts request created in Open Sky (via call center or survey)
2. Approved by parts team
3. Pushed to warehouse system
4. Pick/pack/ship
5. Tracking data flows back to Open Sky
6. Rep sees shipment status on mobile

**Volume:**
- Supports all 4,140 reps
- Unknown shipment volume per week
- Mix of parts from CP warehouse and partner warehouses

**Pain Points:**
- Partner warehouse data is manual (spreadsheet upload)
- Parts sometimes ship to store, sometimes to rep's home (inconsistent)
- No proactive parts stocking (all reactive)

## Billing Model

### Service Types & Pricing

**Hourly (Shared Teams):**
- Rep paid hourly rate (varies by role, region)
- Client billed for actual time in store + drive time + mileage
- Strict time boundaries (if project is 60 min, rep stops at 60 min)
- Job costing tracked by project → invoiced to client

**Salary (Dedicated Teams):**
- Rep paid flat salary
- Client billed monthly/quarterly retainer or per-project fee
- Rep has flexibility to explore issues and go off-script
- Still track time for reporting, but not tied to billing

**Per-Diem:**
- Some projects pay flat rate per visit (door fee)
- Rep incentivized to complete quickly
- Used for simple audits or compliance checks

**Alternate Pay Rates:**
- Some stores pay different rates (e.g., higher for remote locations)
- Rep must accept alternate rate via email before assignment

### Expense Reimbursement

**Mileage:**
- Auto-calculated by Open Sky (store-to-store routing)
- Reimbursed via ADP payroll

**Travel:**
- Flights, hotels, meals for multi-day projects
- Booked by dedicated travel team via Agency platform
- Frequent last-minute changes

**Other Expenses:**
- Rep submits via mobile app (photo of receipt)
- Tied to specific visit (job costing)
- Manager approval workflow

### Job Costing

**Every project has a job number:**
- Created in Open Sky when project is set up
- All time, mileage, expenses, parts tracked to job number
- Flows to Business Central (ERP) for invoicing
- Client can see job-level profitability (after-the-fact, not real-time)

**Pain Points:**
- No real-time project profitability visibility
- Can't see client-level view (only project-level)
- Job costing doesn't prevent overages (only tracks them)

## Organizational Structure

### Lines of Business (Siloed)

Each LOB operates somewhat independently:
- **Merchandising** (largest, ~2,500 reps, 60 field managers, 30 client service managers)
- **Break-Fix** (~500 reps)
- **Construction/Installation** (~300 reps)
- **Dedicated Brand Teams** (~800 reps across LG, Samsung, Microsoft, etc.)
- **Audits & Compliance** (mix)

**Current State:**
- Separate hierarchies per LOB
- Separate project managers / client service teams
- Separate field operations / supervisor teams
- Limited cross-pollination

**Future State (Next 2-3 Months):**
- Shifting to **geographic model** (away from LOB silos)
- Want "fluid resources" who can work across multiple clients/work types
- Requires changes to: incentives, training, systems, reporting hierarchy

### Roles & Responsibilities

**Client Service / Project Managers:**
- Set up projects in Open Sky
- Build surveys (question sets, conditional logic)
- Configure waves (date range, store count, time in store)
- Manage store assignments (bucket tool)
- Billing and client relationships
- NOT responsible for field operations

**Account Team / Field Operations / Supervisors:**
- Manage field reps (payroll review, schedule compliance, performance)
- Bulk rescheduling when plans break
- Address rep issues (equipment, training, conflicts)
- NOT responsible for project setup or client relationships

**Hybrid (Small Dedicated Teams):**
- One person does both client service + field ops
- More common on dedicated brand teams

**Support Center / Call Center:**
- Troubleshoot issues (system problems, work guidance, logistics)
- Parts ordering and approval
- Escalate complex issues to IT or field managers

**Parts Fulfillment Team:**
- Triage parts requests (approve/reject)
- Manage warehouse operations
- Track shipments and delivery status

**Travel Team:**
- Book flights, hotels, rental cars
- Coordinate multi-day project logistics
- Handle last-minute changes

**Learning Team:**
- Manage LMS (third-party platform)
- Assign required courses by project
- Track compliance

**Data Services (James Dyer's Team):**
- Extract data from Open Sky (4-hour batch)
- Load to data warehouse
- Build Tableau/PowerBI dashboards
- Join Open Sky data with financial systems, payroll, etc.

## Competitive Landscape

**No direct competitors mentioned in reverse demo.**

They built Open Sky internally after consolidating 6 acquired companies. Likely evaluated:
- ServiceMax
- ClickSoftware (now part of Salesforce FSL)
- FieldOne
- FieldAware
- Other workforce management platforms

**Why they built custom:**
- Extreme survey flexibility requirements
- Multi-LOB complexity (merch + break-fix + construction + dedicated teams)
- Consolidation of 3 legacy platforms with different workflows
- Self-scheduling culture + hard scheduling requirements
- Unique billing models (hourly, salary, per-diem, door fee, alternate rates)

**Why they might replace Open Sky:**
- Maintenance burden (custom code, limited vendor support)
- Can't scale AI/intelligent scheduling capabilities
- 4-hour data latency can't be reduced without major rearchitecture
- Mobile stability issues across device fragmentation
- Talent hard to find (custom platform, not widely used skill set)

## Risk Factors

### Technical Risks

1. **Open Sky rollout (July 6)** — if it goes poorly, delays decision; if it goes well, reduces urgency
2. **Mobile offline requirement** — non-negotiable, FSL mobile must match or beat Open Sky
3. **Survey flexibility** — Open Sky surveys are extremely configurable; FSL must match
4. **Integration complexity** — ADP, Business Central, LMS, WMS, travel platform, Freshdesk
5. **Data latency** — 4-hour batch → real-time is a major architectural lift

### Business Risks

1. **Budget consumed by stabilization** — if July rollout has issues, no budget for Salesforce
2. **Self-scheduling culture** — moving to FSL's scheduling model may face rep resistance
3. **Org structure in flux** — LOB silos → geographic model (next 2-3 months)
4. **Pilot may fail** — if 70-license crawl doesn't work, no expansion
5. **Competing priorities** — AI investments, other platform upgrades

### Competitive Risks

1. **ServiceMax** — has retail execution capabilities, strong in field service
2. **ClickSoftware** — was a competitor, now part of Salesforce (risk mitigated)
3. **Build vs. Buy** — they already built Open Sky; might continue custom development
